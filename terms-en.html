  • If you are under 14, you may not share videos or send reports without the consent of a parent or legal guardian.

3. Sharing videos and photos

When you set a video or photo for a song with "Share with everyone" turned on, the file and how you matched it to the song are uploaded to the operator's server, and it becomes the wallpaper on the Macs of other users who play the same song. This box is on by default. If you turn it off, the file stays on your Mac only, and the box stays off the next time.

By sharing, you promise the following.

  1. You only upload what you have the right to upload — something you made yourself, something the rights holder allowed you to use, or something whose term of protection has ended. Crediting the source is not permission.
  2. You do not upload: anything that infringes someone else's copyright, likeness or trademark; pornography or child sexual abuse material; anything that harasses a person or exposes their private life; anything that incites violence; or anything else the law prohibits.
  3. For what you share, you give the operator a free, non-exclusive license to store it, send it to other users and show it inside the app (including previews in the Discover list). The license ends when you stop sharing. The copyright stays with you.
  4. You can stop sharing at any time in the app window. When you do, the file is deleted from the server, and it is removed from the Macs of users who already received it the next time their app checks the list (usually within about 10 minutes, while the app is running).

When you share, the app uploads a copy with the audio removed. Large videos are compressed again at the same resolution and frame rate. For videos above 4K or above 60 frames per second, the app also makes a smaller version for screens that can't show the full one, and uploads a small still image for the Discover list. The original on your Mac is not changed.

There are limits: 200 MB per file, and 100 items and 5 GB per account. When the server's storage is full, new uploads may be refused for a while. The operator may change these numbers.

Videos and photos brought in from the web (section 4) show the same box, on by default, and the same promises apply — if you don't have the right to upload it, turn the box off.

4. Bringing things in from the web

The app can download a video or photo from a web page you point it at and set it as your wallpaper. Your Mac downloads it directly from that site; it does not pass through the operator's server. Whether to share it is decided by the box in section 3, and the address you brought it from is never uploaded. You are responsible for following the terms of the service that runs that page and for respecting the rights in its content. The app does not fetch anything that requires signing in or that is technically protected against copying (DRM).

5. Copyright reports and takedowns

The operator does not screen uploads in advance. It handles reports instead.

  • A rights holder (or their agent) can select the video in the app and click "Report…", or email the contact below. A request must include (1) the sender's name and a contact email (and the rights holder's name, if sent by an agent), (2) the work said to be infringed, (3) material showing that the sender holds the rights, (4) which video it is, and (5) a statement that the request is true.
  • When a report is made in the app, the video is taken down right away, before anyone decides whether it infringes (if the same video is reported repeatedly, or many reports arrive at once, the operator looks first). The operator checks whether the request is complete. If it is, the video stays down and the operator notifies the person who made the request and the uploader. If it is not, the video is restored. Because uploaders are anonymous, they are told in the app window: "Taken down after a report".
  • The uploader may, within 30 days of learning of the takedown, send a "Request reinstatement…" from the app window together with material showing they have the right to upload. The operator tells the rights holder the planned date of reinstatement (between 7 and 14 days after receiving the request) and restores the video unless the rights holder reports by that date that they have filed a lawsuit.
  • Anyone who makes a report or a reinstatement request without the right to do so must compensate for the damage it causes (Korean Copyright Act, Article 103(6)).
  • Accounts that are taken down repeatedly are blocked from uploading. The threshold is three complete reports from different rights holders.
  • Problems other than copyright (sexual or violent content and so on) can be reported in the same window as "Something else". The video also comes down right away; the operator then judges it against section 3 and restores it if it does not break those rules.
  • If you don't want videos and photos uploaded by other users to appear on your desktop automatically, turn off "Get videos other people shared" in Settings.
